Approach

How a project runs.

Four stages, from the first conversation to a system your school relies on every day. The last one does not end.

  1. 01

    Understand

    We sit with the people doing the work, not a requirements document about them, and map where time is lost.

  2. 02

    Design

    We design every screen around a real moment in someone's day, and check it with them before we build.

  3. 03

    Build

    We build in short steps you can see and try, and test on the phones and laptops your people actually use.

  4. 04

    Look after it

    We maintain, improve and extend the system as your school changes. Launch is a milestone, not the end.

After launch

We stay responsible for what we build.

Once a system is live it becomes part of how your school runs, and it needs someone accountable for it. That is us.

  • Maintenance and bug fixes
  • Security updates
  • Hosting and backups
  • Support from the people who built it
  • New features as your needs change
Questions

What working with us looks like.

With a conversation about the process, not the software. We want to know who is involved, what happens at each step and where time is lost. From there we can tell you whether a system is worth building and roughly what it would involve.

Usually not. Most schools already run tools that do their job well. Often our work is connecting them and removing the typing-in-twice between them.

Most in the first two stages, where your input shapes the system, and much less while we build. We would rather ask for a few focused sessions early than a constant stream of meetings.

We remain responsible for it: maintenance, fixes, security updates, hosting, backups, support and new features. These arrangements are agreed in writing before launch.

That is normal. Schools change, and a system that cannot keep up stops being used. We build so that changes can be made quickly.

Ownership and access are agreed in writing at the start of every project, so it is always clear what belongs to your school.
